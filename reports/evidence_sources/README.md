# Evidence sources — 2026-11-08

Exploratory checks on where the November text gain sits and how far the equivalence screen can be trusted.
No model was refitted. Generated by `python -m src.evidence_sources` (`src/evidence_sources.py`, tests in
`tests/test_evidence_sources.py`) from the reviewed benchmark's saved slices, the private feature cache and the
private benchmark predictions. Aggregates only: `evidence_sources.json`. No independent review. All of these
questions were chosen after the results were known.

## Effective clusters

All 1,453,840 eligible records sit in 48 countries, but they behave like
**5.7 effective countries** (inverse Herfindahl); 10 countries hold 80% of records. That is a descriptive
index, not a formal effective-cluster estimate. It helps explain why clustering changed so many August conclusions.

**The concentration hypothesis failed.** Users of these terms are not more concentrated than records overall:
urgency spans 7.1 effective countries and first person 11.3, against 5.7 for all records. The unresolved status is
better attributed to how few records use them. This check cannot show whether slopes also differ across countries.

## Equivalence stress test

The screen uses t with 47 df. Repeating it with t(4), from the effective-country index, keeps
**21 of 23** bounded results, BH-adjusted within each family as before. Lost:
basic needs (programme-adjusted, 24-hour); sentiment (programme-adjusted, 24-hour).
All five duration verdicts for family, basic needs, business, agency and third person hold in both specifications.
This is a heuristic stress test on unchanged standard errors, not a calibrated few-cluster method.

## Where the linear text gain sits

Gain is S minus S+M+L+R MAE in days; positive is better. Leave-one-out covers countries with a saved slice.

| Year | Gain, all records | Gain, described records | Records with no description | Rows with unseen exact template | Gain on those rows | Countries with a gain | Gain leaving one country out | Largest contributor |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 2021 | 0.293 | 0.302 | 6,231 | 96.0% | 0.302 | 21 of 30 | 0.262–0.385 | PH |
| 2022 | 0.372 | 0.378 | 3,360 | 97.3% | 0.379 | 23 of 29 | 0.277–0.586 | KE |
| 2023 | 0.259 | 0.364 | 48 | 99.8% | 0.365 | 18 of 28 | 0.155–0.401 | KE |
| 2024 | 0.389 | 0.389 | 19 | 99.8% | 0.388 | 22 of 27 | 0.164–0.502 | KE |
| 2025 | 0.260 | 0.260 | 5 | 99.2% | 0.258 | 11 of 24 | 0.169–0.312 | PH |

1. **No single country carries the gain.** Removing any one saved country leaves it positive in every year.
   It is still uneven: Kenya or the Philippines is the largest contributor each year, and Tajikistan loses in all five.
2. **Exact template reuse does not explain it.** At least 99.2% of described evaluation rows use a digit-normalised
   template unseen in training, and they carry the gain. This cannot rule out near-duplicate templates, which an exact
   signature misses, so "text may encode programme templates" stays as a caveat.
3. **2023 was understated by 48 records.** Those records have no description, and predictions on them reach
   22,436 days. Without them the 2023 gain is 0.364, not 0.259. The 0.26–0.39 range does not change.
4. **In 2024–2025 the gain is on fast loans.** It is positive below 7 days and negative above, for example 2025:
   0.75 days at 0–1 days and −1.21 over 30 days. This pattern does not hold in 2021–2022. Bands are defined by
   the outcome, so this is descriptive and cannot be used to segment loans in advance.

None of this measures what editing a description does.
