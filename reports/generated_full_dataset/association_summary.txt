Average Within-Region Family-Framing Slopes
==============================================================

An interaction coefficient only tests whether a region_group level's family_mentions_per_100_words slope differs from the reference level's. It does NOT test whether the focal measure is associated with the outcome WITHIN that group - and because the focal term is interacted with several moderators at once, 'main effect + group term' is the slope only at the other moderators' reference levels, one unrepresentative cell. This section reports the correct quantity: each group's slope averaged over that group's own observed composition of the other moderators, as a weighted linear contrast, with standard errors and 95% confidence intervals under both HC3 and country_name-clustered covariance. The clustered p-value and interval use statsmodels' normal reference, which is only trustworthy with many clusters - and a group's slope is identified only by the clusters inside that group. Each row therefore also reports a few-cluster reference: a t distribution with (clusters in group - 1) degrees of freedom on the same clustered SE (Cameron & Miller 2015, sec. VI). For a two-cluster group that is t(1), whose 95% critical value is 12.7 instead of 1.96. A slope that is 'significant' only under the normal reference but not under the few-cluster reference is a descriptive pattern, not a statistically supported association.

Sign conventions are OPPOSITE between the two models: the duration model is log(1 + days), so NEGATIVE = faster funding; the 24-hour model is log-odds of funding within 24h, so POSITIVE = faster.

A group's slope is identified only by the clusters within it; a group containing very few clusters cannot separate the focal association from whatever else is idiosyncratic about those specific clusters, and its estimate pools the whole group - it is not evidence about any individual constituent cluster.
